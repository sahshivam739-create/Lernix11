🌍 Gravitation

Class 11 Physics • Lernix11

1. Universal Law of Gravitation

🌍 ↔️ 🌙

📖 Definition

Every two objects in the universe attract each other with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.

F = Gm₁m₂ / r²
⭐ Important:

Gravitational force is always attractive and acts along the line joining the centres of the two bodies.

2. Acceleration Due to Gravity

🍎 ⬇️ 🌍

📖 Definition

The acceleration produced in a freely falling body due to the gravitational attraction of Earth is called acceleration due to gravity.

g = GM / R²
⭐ Near Earth's surface:

g ≈ 9.8 m/s²

3. Variation of g with Height

🌍 ⬆️ 🚀

📖 Concept

The value of acceleration due to gravity decreases as height above Earth's surface increases.

gₕ = g (R / (R+h))²

4. Variation of g with Depth

🌍 ⬇️ 📍

📖 Concept

The value of g decreases as we move towards the centre of Earth.

gd = g(1 - d/R)
⭐ At Earth's centre:

g = 0

5. Gravitational Potential

🌍 ⚡ 📍

📖 Definition

Gravitational potential at a point is the work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to that point.

V = -GM/r

6. Gravitational Potential Energy

🛰️ 🌍 ⚡

📖 Definition

Gravitational potential energy is the energy possessed by a body because of its position in a gravitational field.

U = -GMm/r
⭐ Near Earth's surface:

U = mgh

7. Escape Velocity

🚀 🌍 ⬆️

📖 Definition

Escape velocity is the minimum speed required for an object to escape from the gravitational field of a planet without further propulsion.

vₑ = √(2GM/R)

For Earth: vₑ ≈ 11.2 km/s

8. Orbital Velocity

🛰️ 🔄 🌍

📖 Definition

Orbital velocity is the speed required by a satellite to remain in a stable circular orbit around a planet.

vₒ = √(GM/r)
⭐ Important:

For a satellite close to Earth's surface, vₒ is approximately 7.9 km/s.

9. Satellites

🛰️ 🌍 🔄

📖 Concept

A satellite is a body that revolves around a planet or another celestial body under gravitational attraction.

⭐ Types:

Natural satellites and artificial satellites.

10. Geostationary Satellite

🛰️ 📡 🌍

📖 Definition

A geostationary satellite appears stationary relative to an observer on Earth.

⭐ Conditions:

It moves in a circular equatorial orbit in the same direction as Earth's rotation and has a period of about 24 hours.

11. Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

🪐 🔄 ☀️

📖 Three Laws

First Law: Planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus.

Second Law: The line joining the planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time.

Third Law: The square of the time period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.

T² ∝ a³
