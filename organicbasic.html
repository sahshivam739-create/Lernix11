1. Introduction to Organic Chemistry

🧪 ⚛️ 🧬

📖 Definition

Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry that deals mainly with carbon compounds, especially hydrocarbons and their derivatives.

⭐ Important:

Carbon shows tetravalency and catenation, which allow it to form a very large number of compounds.

2. Tetravalency of Carbon

C 🔗 🔗 🔗 🔗

📖 Concept

Carbon has four valence electrons and generally forms four covalent bonds to complete its octet.

3. Catenation

C-C-C-C-C

📖 Definition

The ability of carbon atoms to form bonds with other carbon atoms and make chains or rings is called catenation.

4. Classification of Organic Compounds

🔗 🔄 ⭕
⭐ Main Types:

Open-chain compounds

Cyclic compounds

Aromatic compounds

Heterocyclic compounds

5. Functional Group

⚛️ 🔗 🧪

📖 Definition

An atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic chemical properties of an organic compound is called a functional group.

⭐ Examples:

−OH → Alcohol
−CHO → Aldehyde
>C=O → Ketone
−COOH → Carboxylic acid
−NH₂ → Amine

6. Homologous Series

🧪 ➡️ 🧪 ➡️ 🧪

📖 Definition

A homologous series is a group of organic compounds having the same functional group and general formula, where successive members differ by −CH₂−.

7. IUPAC Nomenclature

🔤 🧪 🔢

📖 Concept

IUPAC nomenclature provides systematic rules for naming organic compounds.

⭐ Basic Steps:

1. Select the longest carbon chain.

2. Number the chain.

3. Identify substituents.

4. Identify the principal functional group.

5. Write the complete name.

8. Isomerism

🔄 🧪 🔄

📖 Definition

Isomerism is the phenomenon in which compounds have the same molecular formula but different structures or arrangements.

⭐ Main Types:

Structural isomerism
Stereoisomerism

9. Structural Isomerism

🧪 🔀 🧪
⭐ Types:

Chain isomerism

Position isomerism

Functional isomerism

Metamerism

10. Electronic Effects

⚡ ➡️ ⚛️

📖 Concept

Electronic effects explain how electrons are distributed in organic molecules and how this affects their stability and reactivity.

⭐ Main Effects:

Inductive effect
Resonance
Electromeric effect
Hyperconjugation

11. Inductive Effect

δ+ ➡️ δ−

📖 Definition

Inductive effect is the permanent displacement of sigma electrons along a carbon chain due to electronegativity differences.

⭐ Types:

+I effect → Electron releasing

−I effect → Electron withdrawing

12. Resonance

🔄 ⚛️ 🔄

📖 Concept

Resonance occurs when a molecule or ion can be represented by two or more contributing structures differing only in electron arrangement.

⭐ Important:

The actual molecule is represented by a resonance hybrid.

13. Electromeric Effect

⚡ ⇄ ⚛️

📖 Definition

Electromeric effect is the complete transfer of a shared electron pair of a multiple bond to one atom in the presence of an attacking reagent.

14. Hyperconjugation

⚛️ 🔗 ⚡

📖 Concept

Hyperconjugation involves delocalisation of sigma electrons, usually from a C−H bond adjacent to a multiple bond or electron-deficient centre.

15. Reaction Intermediates

⚡ 🧪 ⚛️
⭐ Main Intermediates:

Carbocation

Carbanion

Free radical

Carbene

16. Carbocation

C⁺ ⚛️

📖 Definition

A carbocation is an organic species in which a carbon atom carries a positive charge.

⭐ General Stability:

3° > 2° > 1° > methyl

17. Carbanion

C⁻ ⚛️

📖 Definition

A carbanion is an organic species in which a carbon atom carries a negative charge.

⭐ General Stability:

Methyl > 1° > 2° > 3°

18. Free Radical

C • ⚡

📖 Definition

A free radical is a species containing an unpaired electron.

⭐ General Stability:

3° > 2° > 1° > methyl

20. Qualitative Analysis

🔬 🧪 🔍

📖 Concept

Qualitative analysis is used to detect the elements present in an organic compound.

⭐ Common Elements:

Carbon • Hydrogen • Nitrogen • Sulphur • Halogens

21. Quantitative Analysis

⚖️ 🔢 🧪

📖 Concept

Quantitative analysis determines the percentage amount of different elements present in an organic compound.
